No positive transfer to unfamiliar tool use from skill-curated trajectories: with-skill 61.2% vs no-skill 62.1% on BFCL v4 (single-seed Qwen3-8B LoRA pilot)

Question

SkillsBench trajectories can be collected with or without curated skill files in the agent's environment. We asked whether fine-tuning Qwen3-8B on successful trajectories collected with skills transfers better to unfamiliar tool use than fine-tuning on matched successful trajectories collected without skills. The pilot tested four things:

  1. Transfer. Does the with-skill arm beat the no-skill arm on the expanded BFCL v4 function-calling benchmark (2,190 items, tools unseen in training)?
  2. Preservation. Do the trained arms hold base-model capability on IFEval, a fixed 500-item MMLU slice, and a tool-restraint probe?
  3. Cleanliness. Is the training corpus free of eval contamination and residual explicit skill text?
  4. Agentic holdout. Does the difference show up on a predeclared 21-task Toolathlon subset? (This leg returned no data; see the Toolathlon section.)

Transfer to unfamiliar tool use

  • No positive short-horizon transfer detected. On BFCL v4 (2,190 shared items) the with-skill arm scored 61.23% vs no-skill's 62.10%: paired Δ = −0.87pp, 95% CI [−1.92, +0.18]. The interval includes zero.
  • Both trained arms are detectably below base on BFCL. No-skill − base = −1.19pp [−2.33, −0.05]; with-skill − base = −2.05pp [−3.15, −0.96]. This is a short-horizon function-calling result; long-horizon transfer is unmeasured.
  • Preservation is tied. IFEval strict 81.33 / 80.96 / 80.96% and MMLU 50.4 / 51.0 / 51.4% (base / no-skill / with-skill) are statistically indistinguishable, and no arm makes unnecessary tool calls on the restraint probe (rate 0 for all three, n = 120 pairs).
  • The null is low-power, not a refutation. One seed, 238 matched training rows per arm (604,737 supervised tokens, 12.1% of the planned 5M-token cap), and two corpus asymmetries that plausibly bias toward null (see the corpus audit).

Task-paired bootstrap—not overlap of the displayed per-arm Wilson intervals—is the inferential test: with-skill minus no-skill is −0.87pp [−1.92, +0.18], while with-skill minus base is −2.05pp [−3.15, −0.96]. Per-category accuracy for the 11 BFCL v4 categories, one row per category, one marker per checkpoint (base circle, no-skill diamond, with-skill square). The with-skill arm's deficit is not uniform. Against the no-skill control it gains on (a) parallel calls +1.0pp, (b) simple JavaScript +2.0pp, and (c) simple Python +0.5pp, but loses on multiple functions −1.5pp, parallel multiple −3.0pp, simple Java −3.0pp, and all four multi-turn categories (−0.5 to −1.5pp each). Multi-turn is hard for every checkpoint (16.0–18.0% overall, see Figure 1's stat strip), so the small per-category multi-turn sample sizes (n = 200) make individual category deltas noisy. The mixed texture is consistent with seed-level noise rather than a coherent category-specific effect; a multi-seed run would be needed to separate the two.

Two concrete base-model failures from the official simple-Python score file illustrate what BFCL punishes. Asked for the structure of a human cell, the model called the right function but passed cell_type="human cell" where the scorer accepts only "human". Asked to average a list of integers, it passed integer arrays where the scorer's schema requires float arrays. These examples are illustrative only: they show the benchmark's exact schema/type strictness, they are base-model errors, and they say nothing about the with-skill vs no-skill difference.

Capability preservation

Training moved none of the preservation metrics beyond noise. IFEval prompt-strict (n = 541): base 81.33%, no-skill 80.96%, with-skill 80.96%. IFEval prompt-loose: 85.21%, 84.84%, 84.66%. MMLU fixed-500: 50.4%, 51.0%, 51.4%, all tied within the ~2.24pp per-checkpoint standard error. On the tool-restraint probe (n = 120 paired questions) accuracy with no tools offered was 95.83 / 95.00 / 94.17% and with irrelevant tools offered 94.17 / 92.50 / 95.00%; the unnecessary tool-call rate was 0 for all three checkpoints.

All four benchmark families were normalized to the shared task IDs and analyzed with a task-paired bootstrap (10,000 replicates, seed 17). Figure 4 shows the primary BFCL transfer interval and key preservation intervals; the full three-arm results are in results/full_paired_analysis.json.

Training corpus and audit

The v7 corpus started from 482 reviewed-successful SkillsBench trials, of which 467 parsed. Fifteen were excluded: 5 with suspected compacted histories (all in the with-skill arm) and 10 with indivisible oversized assistant actions (5 per arm). Exact cross-arm matching then produced 238 training row pairs with 604,737 supervised tokens per arm across 34 task groups, plus 105 validation rows (250,476 tokens per arm) and 80 audit rows (165,166 tokens per arm). That is 12.1% of the planned 5M-token-per-arm cap, which is why the null carries pilot weight only.

Skill-text removal was verified two ways: zero residual explicit skill paths and zero task-subtracted 160-character skill chunks survive in the corpus. The guarantee is bounded, though. Arbitrary alignment of the scanning windows only guarantees detection of verbatim quotes of about 239 characters or longer; shorter verbatim quotes and paraphrases of skill content may remain in the with-skill arm's trajectories.

Two corpus asymmetries plausibly bias this pilot toward a null. Selected rows contain 55 loss-masked redaction-marker tokens in the no-skill arm vs 583 in the with-skill arm, and the 5 compacted-history exclusions all came from the with-skill arm. Both remove or mask more with-skill content than no-skill content, which could dilute a real skill-derived signal.

Training used LoRA rank 16, α = 32, dropout 0.05 (43.65M trainable parameters), one pass over the data, seed 17, identical for both arms. The training-input condition audit passed for both jobs.

Figure 5: Validation loss over training, both arms

Validation loss at optimizer steps 7–30 for the two training arms (105 held-out rows and 250,476 supervised tokens per arm; 30 optimizer steps total, seed 17 for both).

Both arms train normally: validation loss falls from 0.891 to 0.750 (no-skill) and 0.919 to 0.785 (with-skill) and flattens by step 28. Single run per arm, no uncertainty estimate.

Contamination audit

An expanded overlap audit compared the training corpus against all 3,372 eval items (BFCL, IFEval, MMLU, tool restraint). It found zero exact instruction matches, zero exact token 5-gram matches, and zero exact canonical tool-schema matches. The 95th-percentile token 5-gram Jaccard similarity is 0 and the maximum is 0.00585. Eleven eval items share a single tool name with the corpus, the weakest possible form of overlap. The transfer numbers above are not explained by train–eval leakage. Full audit: artifact://han-solo-757b7c/experiments/exp_01ky0ztpc9eqn9g67e30pjt5k9/analysis/overlap_v2/summary.json.

Toolathlon: unavailable, not failed

The predeclared 21-task Toolathlon subset produced no data. The official evaluation service returned busy on seven clean admission attempts; no submission was accepted, and no preprocessing, model execution, or verification ran. There is consequently no Toolathlon score and no qualitative claim in either direction. The status is unavailable, not a failed benchmark.

What this pilot settles

  • Answer: no positive short-horizon transfer detected. With-skill minus no-skill is −0.87pp on BFCL with paired 95% CI [−1.92, +0.18]. Both LoRAs are detectably below base on BFCL; long-horizon transfer remains unmeasured because Toolathlon was unavailable.
  • The null is low-power. One seed and 12.1% of the planned token budget; a real effect of a few points in either direction is compatible with these intervals. This is a pilot reading, not a refutation of skill-trajectory transfer.
  • Two biases point toward null. The 55 vs 583 loss-masked marker-token asymmetry and the five with-skill-only compaction exclusions both remove more with-skill content; residual sub-239-character skill quotes or paraphrases could also blur the arm contrast in the opposite direction. Neither is quantified.
  • Preservation is clean. Training at this scale cost nothing measurable on IFEval, MMLU, or tool restraint, so a larger-budget rerun does not risk obvious capability damage.
  • What would change the reading: a multi-seed run at or near the 5M-token cap and a functioning long-horizon agentic holdout. Toolathlon remains unanswered, not negative.
